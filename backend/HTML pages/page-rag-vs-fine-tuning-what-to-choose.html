Plain answer

What is RAG? What is Fine-Tuning?

Quick answer

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is a technique that retrieves relevant documents from a knowledge base and feeds them into an LLM at inference time to ground responses in external facts. Fine-tuning updates all or part of an LLM's weights on a domain-specific dataset to internalize patterns, style, or knowledge without external retrieval. Both are open-source compatible, and the choice depends on whether your bottleneck is access to information (RAG) or adaptation of behavior (fine-tuning).

The hybrid pattern

The most effective production systems often combine both: fine-tune a base model for domain-specific tone and formatting, then layer RAG for factual grounding. This hybrid approach is common in 2026 for applications that need both personality and accuracy.

The honest breakdown

Where RAG vs fine-tuning wins

RAG WINS WHEN

Retrieval quality is the bottleneck

  • You need access to dynamic, frequently updated data without retraining: RAG retrieves from live corpora, while fine-tuning requires full retraining cycles
  • Factual accuracy and traceability are critical: RAG can cite source documents, fine-tuning cannot guarantee provenance and often hallucinates more
  • Your knowledge base is large and structured: RAG scales to millions of documents with no additional training cost, fine-tuning becomes prohibitively expensive at scale
  • You want to ship faster with less upfront data: RAG works with as few as 10 documents, fine-tuning requires thousands of high-quality examples for meaningful gains
FINE-TUNE WINS WHEN

Training data captures implicit knowledge

  • You need to teach the model a new writing style, tone, or domain-specific language that retrieval cannot replicate
  • Your use case involves consistent, repetitive patterns (e.g., code generation for a proprietary API, medical report summarization) where fine-tuning reduces latency by eliminating retrieval steps
  • You have a high-quality labeled dataset of at least 1,000 examples and the compute budget for periodic retraining
  • Retrieval latency or cost is a bottleneck: fine-tuned models can generate answers faster by embedding knowledge directly into weights, avoiding a retrieval step

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When should I choose fine-tuning over RAG?
Choose fine-tuning when your model needs to adopt a specific tone, format, or domain behavior that RAG's retrieved context cannot enforce. RAG is superior for factual recall and updating knowledge without retraining.
Can RAG and fine-tuning be used together?
Yes, this is the most powerful pattern: fine-tune the model for style and instruction following, then use RAG to supply the factual context. This hybrid approach addresses both behavioral and retrieval challenges.
How do you decide between RAG and fine-tuning for my project?
We evaluate where your LLM's failures originate: if it's missing context, RAG is the answer; if it's poor output structure or behavior, fine-tuning is required. The decision is made during discovery and delivered as a written recommendation.
Is fine-tuning more expensive than RAG?
Fine-tuning has higher upfront cost due to data preparation and GPU compute, but lower per-query inference cost. RAG has lower initial cost but ongoing vector database and LLM token expenses. The total cost of ownership depends on query volume and update frequency.
